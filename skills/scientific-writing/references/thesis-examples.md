# Thesis Exposition: Source Examples

Complete local PDFs and searchable extracts for all six dissertations are linked in [source-library.md](source-library.md#dissertations).

Use these examples when a thesis needs fuller explanation, clearer ownership or stronger connections between studies. They calibrate editorial decisions; they are not instructions from the authors, a universal style, or scientific evidence for another manuscript. Read only the examples relevant to the problem being edited.

The dissertation authors wrote these texts. Sutton's supervision or endorsement does not make every passage his writing. Strong claims in a source must not be imported into the user's work. The user's journal-paper mode remains `we`, even when a source thesis uses `I`.

## Shibhansh Dohare — explain why the parts of a method exist

[*Learning Forever using Artificial Neural Networks* (2026)](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/Dohare_Shibhansh_PhD.pdf).

- **Abstract, pp. ii–iii; preface, p. iv:** first-person actions coexist with explicit collaborative attribution. Applying this to a thesis requires the student's contribution record; it is not a global pronoun substitution.
- **Section 6.1, pp. 54–55:** the method explanation shows how one operation creates a problem that another choice addresses. This is more informative than listing components. Explain the relevant dependency before presenting the complete algorithm.
- **Section 6.2, pp. 59–63:** parameters are explained through an interpretable count, an equation and numerical cases. Use examples when they make a parameter's effect understandable.
- **Chapter 5, pp. 46–52:** candidate definitions are examined through cases they fail to capture. A definition can be motivated and bounded rather than introduced as self-evident.

## Juan Fernando Hernandez Garcia — make the comparison interpretable

[*Selective Reinitialization Algorithms for Preventing Plasticity Loss in Artificial Neural Networks* (2026)](http://www.incompleteideas.net/papers/FernandoPhD.pdf).

- **Section 3.1, pp. 15–16:** possible diagnostic patterns are described before the actual mixed trajectory is interpreted. Explain what would count as evidence when the interpretation is not obvious.
- **Section 5.4, pp. 82–84:** a comparison changes more than the intended variable; the text identifies that problem and describes an approximately matched comparison. Make the reason for the control visible.
- **Sections 6.3 and 7.4, pp. 100–103 and 121–123:** divergent runs and changed denominators remain explicit. Conditional performance and overall reliability must not be conflated.
- **Chapter 4 ending and Chapter 5 opening, pp. 61–64:** a remaining methodological constraint motivates the next chapter. Transfer this relationship only where it actually holds for the user's projects.

## Gautham Vasan — integrate contributions without overstating integration

[*Robots That Learn on the Fly Through Real-World Interaction* (2026)](https://gauthamvasan.com/papers/Vasan_Gautham_202609_PhD.pdf). This is an additional comparison thesis shared by Sutton, who identified himself as a committee member; do not label it a Sutton-supervised dissertation without separate evidence.

- **Section 1.1, pp. 6–8:** several contributions address named practical requirements. A thesis can explain its coherence through complementary questions without inventing a single pipeline.
- **Chapter 2, pp. 22 and 33; Chapter 6, pp. 99–102:** mathematical formulations and implementation departures are distinguished. State when an approximation changes the scope of a theoretical property.
- **Chapter 5, pp. 92–93:** a simplification transfers some effort to another decision. Report the remaining cost rather than claiming it disappeared.
- **Chapter 7, pp. 125–129:** separately demonstrated contributions are distinguished from their untested combination and the longer-term ambition. Preserve this boundary when making a thesis conclusion more assertive.

## Adam White — separate the desired outcome from an internal diagnostic

[*Developing a predictive approach to knowledge* (2015)](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~amw8/phd.pdf). Sutton supervision is identified in [White's CV, p. 1](https://adamwhite.ca/cv.pdf) and the thesis acknowledgments.

Chapter 7, pp. 98–117, distinguishes an internal approximation from prediction quality, then uses a counterexample to bound the conclusion. Explain the relevance of a diagnostic and evaluate the desired outcome separately. Do not merge both into an unspecified improvement in performance.

## A. Rupam Mahmood — explain what a repair preserves

[*Incremental Off-policy Reinforcement Learning Algorithms* (2017)](http://incompleteideas.net/papers/MahmoodPhD.pdf). [Mahmood's CV, p. 1](https://armahmood.github.io/files/rupam-cv.pdf), verifies Sutton as advisor.

Chapters 9–10, pp. 121–142, distinguish related failure mechanisms, include a matched case where the problem does not arise, and explain a repair through the part of an update it preserves and the part it changes. Distinguish the mathematical object from its computable estimator and specify the level of any stability or equivalence claim. The preface credits Sutton with composing the source manuscript for these chapters; it also records other contributions and thesis additions.

## David Silver — investigate a reversal before explaining it

[*Reinforcement Learning and Simulation-Based Search in Computer Go* (2009)](http://incompleteideas.net/papers/Silver-phd-thesis.pdf). The [ICAPS 2016 programme, p. 15](https://icaps16.icaps-conference.org/Programme.pdf), verifies Sutton supervision.

Chapter 10, especially pp. 104 and 115–116, separates standalone policy quality from performance inside search. It checks a possible comparison confound before developing an explanation and retains a competing method's specific advantage. Explain anomalies through actual checks, not an attractive causal story. Silver explicitly uses `we` to acknowledge collaboration; thesis first-person singular is a user preference, not a universal rule derived from these sources.

## What to transfer into papers

Operational explanations, clear controls, interpretable metrics, stable terminology, adverse findings and calibrated conclusions are useful in both genres. Adapt explanatory length to the paper's readers and venue. Remove thesis roadmaps, unrelated chapter contributions and individual doctoral narration. Use `we` or a concrete scientific subject in the user's papers; do not carry over thesis `I` or assume every thesis finding belongs to the source paper.

## Reading scope

The full substantive chapter text was reviewed for Dohare pp. 1–109, Hernandez Garcia pp. 1–131 and Vasan pp. 1–129, with selected or complete substantive appendices. For the additional dissertations, the reading covered introductions, complete selected technical chapters and conclusions: White pp. 1–8, 96–117 and 153–158; Mahmood pp. 3–9, 25–28, 121–142 and 167–170; Silver pp. 1–8 and 101–119. The additional three were not read in full. This was an exposition review, not independent validation of equations, plots, experiments or every cited source. Page locations are printed page numbers.
