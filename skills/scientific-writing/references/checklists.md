# Editorial Review Prompts

Apply only the prompts relevant to the document and requested edit. They identify reader difficulties or missing information; they are not mandatory paragraph templates or automatic rejection criteria. Do not invent an experiment, fact or interpretation to satisfy a prompt.

## Document and authorship

- Is the artifact treated as a thesis, paper, book chapter, proposal, review, rebuttal or caption?
- For a chapter or review abstract, are the subject, scope and central conclusion clear, with examples kept in proportion and the actual length, format and keyword requirements followed?
- In a thesis, do `I` and `we` match the contribution record? In the user's papers, is the authors' voice `we` rather than a carried-over thesis `I`?
- Does applying or evaluating a collaborator's method remain distinct from inventing it?
- Are completed work, prepared resources, proposed integration and future experiments accurately distinguished?

## Explanation

- Can the reader identify the scientific object and question?
- Are unfamiliar concepts explained before they carry the argument?
- Are intermediate steps present where the reasoning needs them?
- Are consequential method choices explained, including relationships among components?
- Do equations and parameters have the interpretation needed by this reader?
- Does a chapter transition explain a real scientific relationship?
- Are good existing passages preserved rather than rewritten merely for stylistic uniformity?

## Evidence and comparison

- Is it clear what changes, what stays fixed and why the control answers the question?
- Are the task, population, labels, metric and comparison appropriate to the claim as described?
- Are selection rules, resources, aggregation, uncertainty and relevant exclusions visible?
- Are failed runs or conditional analyses given their proper denominators?
- Does a proposed explanation remain distinct from a demonstrated mechanism?
- Are adverse results, exceptions and useful null comparisons retained?
- Are apparent equivalence and generalization claims supported at the stated scope?

## Prose

- Does a vague phrase conceal an operation, observation or specific limitation?
- Are terms stable and pronoun referents clear?
- Is the sentence compressed beyond what a reader can follow?
- Do consecutive short sentences describe one idea whose relationships would be clearer with coordinating, relative or subordinate clauses?
- Can prose colons become full stops or integrated clauses without creating choppy prose or changing citation scope, and are any retained prose colons needed for a formal list?
- Do semicolons connect closely related clauses, and do one-line aphorisms interrupt the explanation?
- Are qualifications placed where they matter, with unnecessary repeats removed?
- Has a stock gap, contrast, caveat or grand ending been added only to satisfy a pattern?
- Does each passage stop when it has explained its point?

## Preservation after editing

Check the changed text against its source for numerical values, units, labels, direction of effects, denominators, uncertainty, citations, scope, authorship and research status. Distinguish checks actually performed from assumptions. Missing evidence is an issue to flag, not a license to invent details or silently change the study.

For venue-specific compliance and reporting requirements, use `submission-checks.md` only when that work is requested or relevant.
